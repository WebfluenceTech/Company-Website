## Register

**Brand surface.** This is a marketing site for a software engineering firm.
The impression is the product. Type, color, density, and motion should all be
tuned for first-impression credibility, not task completion.

## Who is this for?

Two audiences, in order:

1. CTOs and VPs of Engineering at Series A to D companies, evaluating an
   engineering partner on a laptop between meetings. They have seen a hundred
   agency sites this quarter and are looking for a reason to close the tab.
2. Senior engineers, hiring managers, and recruiters judging the taste of the
   people behind the site. For them the site is a work sample, and they will
   read the source.

## Brand voice, three words

**Measured. Exacting. Unhurried.**

The copy already speaks this way: short declarative sentences, numbers over
adjectives, no exclamation marks. The visual system should sound the same.

## Visual references

Named objects, not adjectives:

- IBM technical manuals of the 1970s (Paul Rand era): grid, ink, one working color.
- Klim Type Foundry specimen pages: type as the hero, hairline rules, air.
- Swiss engineering drawings: numbered figures, annotations, registration marks.
- Stripe's print-like light pages, where dark product screens float on paper.

## Anti-references

Equally named:

- Linear's homepage and its thousand clones: glowing gradients on near-black.
- Generic dark-SaaS "aurora" palettes: cyan-to-violet gradient text on #0a0a12.
- Elementor-class agency templates: icon grids, stock photos, carousel heroes.
- Anything impeccable's own detector calls a slop tell: gradient headings,
  dark glow, cyan-on-dark, Inter-by-default.

## Non-negotiables

- The fictional-company disclosure stays in the footer on every page.
- All metrics remain the same fictional-but-consistent numbers.
- WCAG AA contrast is a floor, not a stretch goal.
- prefers-reduced-motion always yields a fully static, fully legible page.
