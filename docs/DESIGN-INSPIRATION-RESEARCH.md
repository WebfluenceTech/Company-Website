# Design Inspiration Research — Portfolio Company Website

Research for building a premium, realistic fictional software company website that showcases
full-stack skills (React, Next.js, TypeScript, MERN, PostgreSQL, SQL Server, Docker, Flutter,
AI agents, automation, SaaS, CRM systems).

> Note: websites evolve constantly. Treat each entry as "study the current live version" —
> the qualities described are the ones these companies are consistently known for.

---

## 1. The 26 websites worth studying

### AI companies

**1. Anthropic (anthropic.com)**
- *Why:* The benchmark for calm, editorial, trustworthy AI branding. Proves premium ≠ flashy.
- *Standout sections:* Hero (typography-led, almost no imagery), Research/Blog index, Product pages for Claude, Careers.
- *Learn:* Restrained color palette (warm neutrals + one accent), serif/sans pairing, generous whitespace, long-form content design. How to make text itself the visual identity.

**2. OpenAI (openai.com)**
- *Why:* Masters the "research lab meets consumer product" duality.
- *Standout:* Hero video/gradient treatments, product index cards, blog/research layout, changelog-style updates.
- *Learn:* Abstract gradient art direction for AI products, large-type editorial headers, clean navigation across a huge site.

**3. Perplexity (perplexity.ai)**
- *Why:* Fast-growing AI product with a distinctive teal/dark identity and product-first landing.
- *Standout:* Hero with live product embed, Enterprise page, Pricing.
- *Learn:* Putting the actual product UI in the hero instead of an illustration; dark-theme discipline.

**4. Runway (runwayml.com)**
- *Why:* The most cinematic AI site — video-first storytelling.
- *Standout:* Full-bleed video hero, product reels, research pages, customer stories from film studios.
- *Learn:* Using motion/video as the primary asset; minimal chrome so the work speaks.

**5. ElevenLabs (elevenlabs.io)**
- *Why:* Interactive demos embedded directly in marketing pages.
- *Standout:* Hero with playable voice demo, use-case pages, developer/API section, pricing.
- *Learn:* "Try it right here" widgets — hugely relevant if your fictional company sells AI agents. Also excellent docs-meets-marketing structure.

**6. Mistral AI (mistral.ai)**
- *Why:* Strong unconventional brand (pixel-art, bold orange/beige) proving you don't need the default dark-SaaS look.
- *Standout:* Hero, model/product cards, news section.
- *Learn:* Committing to a weird-but-consistent visual system; European editorial feel.

### Developer tools

**7. Linear (linear.app)** ⭐
- *Why:* The single most-copied SaaS site of this era — and still the best executed. Dark, precise, obsessed with craft.
- *Standout:* Hero (product screenshot with glow/perspective), feature sections with scroll-triggered reveals, "Built for the future" narrative pages, changelog, careers ("Why Linear").
- *Learn:* Keyboard-first product storytelling, subtle glassmorphism, purposeful micro-animations, feature sections that alternate copy + annotated UI screenshots, the famous gradient-glow product shot.

**8. Vercel (vercel.com)** ⭐
- *Why:* Your stack IS their stack (Next.js). Best example of technical marketing with black/white minimalism.
- *Standout:* Hero (triangle/gradient motifs), Products grid, Customers page with metric-led case studies, Templates gallery, Blog, Pricing.
- *Learn:* Monochrome + selective gradient accents, code snippets as design elements, performance-metric storytelling ("x% faster builds"), conference-grade typography (Geist font, free to use).

**9. Stripe (stripe.com)** ⭐
- *Why:* Still the gold standard of software marketing sites a decade running.
- *Standout:* Animated gradient hero (WebGL), Products mega-nav, Customers/case studies, Docs entry points, Enterprise pages, Jobs.
- *Learn:* Information architecture at scale, diagram-driven product explanations, the art of making payments (boring) feel premium, world-class footer and navigation design.

**10. Raycast (raycast.com)**
- *Why:* Best-in-class dark UI marketing with delightful details.
- *Standout:* Hero with animated product window, Store/extensions grid, Pro pricing page, changelog.
- *Learn:* Red accent on near-black, spotlight/glow effects, showing a desktop app beautifully in the browser, playful easter eggs that signal craft.

**11. Supabase (supabase.com)**
- *Why:* Open-source dev tool with strong community feel and great docs integration; Postgres-centric like your skills.
- *Standout:* Hero with framework logos, feature tabs (Database/Auth/Storage/Edge Functions), customer stories, pricing table, launch-week campaign pages.
- *Learn:* Green-on-dark identity, tabbed product tours with real code, how to present a database product attractively, "Launch Week" as a marketing device.

**12. Clerk (clerk.com)**
- *Why:* Beautiful component-level product visualization for an auth product.
- *Standout:* Hero with live auth components, docs-quality feature pages, pricing.
- *Learn:* Rendering actual UI components (not screenshots) in marketing pages — very achievable in React and very impressive to technical recruiters.

**13. Resend (resend.com)**
- *Why:* Tiny team, immaculate site; proof that minimal scope executed perfectly beats sprawling sites.
- *Standout:* Hero (3D animated cube/particles), code-first feature sections, customers wall.
- *Learn:* One hero moment done extremely well, DX-focused copywriting, restrained page count.

**14. Railway (railway.com)**
- *Why:* Infrastructure made visual — canvas/diagram aesthetics.
- *Standout:* Hero with animated deployment canvas, feature scroll narrative, pricing calculator.

**15. GitHub (github.com/home)**

## 2. Top 5 to model most closely

1. **Linear** — overall visual language, dark theme, feature-section rhythm, micro-interactions.
2. **Stripe** — information architecture, navigation, case-study structure, footer.
3. **Vercel** — technical credibility, typography, code-as-design, performance storytelling (and it's your literal stack).
4. **Attio** — CRM/product presentation, data-model animations (your CRM background makes this the most defensible thing to showcase).
5. **Mercury** — polish, art direction, trust signals, interactive demo pattern.

These five cover the four things recruiters subconsciously score: craft (Linear), scale-thinking (Stripe), stack fluency (Vercel), domain relevance (Attio), and taste (Mercury).

## 3. Recommended section blueprint (the combination)

Build ONE cohesive dark-theme-primary site (with polished light mode) assembled from best-of patterns:

| Section | Model after | Why for you |
|---|---|---|
| **Navigation** | Stripe (mega-nav) simplified to Linear's minimal bar | Shows IA thinking without bloat |
| **Hero** | Linear glow-screenshot + ElevenLabs live demo widget | An actual working AI-agent demo in the hero is your killer differentiator |
| **Logo/social proof strip** | Vercel / Zapier | Use fictional-but-plausible client marks (see ethics below) |
| **Services/Products grid** | Vercel products + Supabase tabbed tours | Tabs: Web Apps, AI Agents, Automation, CRM Systems, Mobile (Flutter) — maps 1:1 to your CV |
| **Product deep-dive (flagship)** | Attio feature sections + n8n workflow canvas | Present a fictional CRM+AI-agent product with animated data-model and node-graph visuals |
| **Case studies** | Stripe customers + Ramp metrics | 3–4 fictional case studies with concrete metrics, architecture diagrams (Tailscale-style), and stack badges |
| **Interactive demo** | Mercury demo / Clerk live components | A real, working embedded demo (chat agent, workflow runner, or CRM board) built in React |
| **Engineering blog** | Vercel + Anthropic | 4–6 REAL technical posts about building the site/demos — genuinely valuable interview material |
| **Pricing** | Supabase / Framer | Three tiers + enterprise; shows product thinking |
| **Careers** | Linear "Why us" + Ramp culture | Short, values-led; makes the company feel alive |
| **Contact/CTA** | Mercury "Open account" pattern → working contact form | Wire it to a real backend (your MERN/PostgreSQL skills) — recruiters notice working forms |
| **Footer** | Stripe | The most-underrated craft signal on the web |
| **Motion system** | Linear micro-interactions + Framer scroll reveals, used sparingly | Craft, not carnival |

**Why this combination fits your profile:** every section maps to a CV claim. The AI-agent hero demo proves the "AI Agents" line; the CRM product page proves the CRM experience; the workflow canvas proves automation; Next.js + working backend forms prove full-stack MERN/Postgres; a Flutter companion-app section proves mobile. No single reference site covers all of that — which is exactly why a synthesis reads as original rather than cloned.

## 4. Recruiter's-eye evaluation of the strategy

### The strategy is sound — IF disclosed
A fictional company site is a well-respected portfolio format. Recruiters see hundreds of identical Next.js portfolios; a full company site with working product demos stands out dramatically. But execution and honesty determine whether it lands as "impressive" or "deceptive."

### Red flags to avoid
1. **Never imply it's a real employer.** Do not list the fictional company as work experience on your CV/LinkedIn as if employed there. This is the fastest way to fail a background check and lose an offer.
2. **No fake real-company logos.** Don't put Nike/Shopify in your "clients" strip. Invent plausible fictional clients (e.g., "Northwind Logistics") or label the strip "Illustrative."
3. **No fabricated testimonials attributed to real people** — invented names + clearly fictional companies only, or skip testimonials for metric-led case studies.
4. **No stock "team" photos presented as real staff.** Either use illustrated avatars, or an "About" page that tells the truth: built and designed by you.
5. **No fake legal/compliance claims** (SOC2 badges, "trusted by 10,000 companies"). Recruiters in enterprise software will spot it instantly.
6. **Don't clone one site.** Hiring managers know Linear's homepage by heart. A recognizable clone reads as "can copy, can't design." The synthesis approach above avoids this.
7. **Don't ship it half-broken.** A premium-looking site with a dead contact form, layout shift, or 40 Lighthouse score is worse than a modest site that's flawless.

### How to present it ethically
- **Footer disclosure on every page:** "⚡ [Company] is a fictional company created by [Your Name] as a portfolio project. [Link to your GitHub/portfolio]." Small, elegant, honest — it doesn't diminish the work; it reframes it as intentional.
- **On the CV:** list it under *Projects*, e.g. "Designed and built a complete fictional SaaS company website — Next.js 15, TypeScript, PostgreSQL, working AI-agent demo — as a brand/product design study."
- **Make the About page the punchline:** "This entire company — brand, product, copy, engineering — was built by one person. Here's how." Link the repo, the Figma, the blog posts. This turns the disclosure into the most impressive page on the site.
- **Open-source the repo** with a great README, architecture diagram, and clean commit history. Recruiters WILL read the code.

### Maximizing credibility and interview impact
1. **Working demos beat animations.** One real embedded AI agent or CRM board > ten scroll effects.
2. **Real engineering blog posts.** "How I built a streaming AI chat widget in Next.js server components" gives interviewers something concrete to ask about — you control the interview topics.
3. **Perfect the measurables:** Lighthouse 95+, full responsive, dark/light themes, accessibility (keyboard nav, contrast, reduced-motion), OG images, sitemap. These are checkable, and checkable excellence is credibility.
4. **Show the process:** a `/colophon` or "How this was built" page listing stack, design decisions, and tradeoffs signals senior-level thinking.
5. **Custom domain + real deployment** (Vercel) with CI/CD via GitHub Actions — the deployment story is itself a skills demo.
6. **Scope discipline:** a Resend-sized site (6–8 pages) executed flawlessly beats a Stripe-sized site executed 70%.

### Bottom line
Positioned as *"I built an entire software company — brand, website, and working product demos — to demonstrate what I can ship end-to-end,"* this is a strong, memorable, ethical portfolio play. Positioned as a real company, it's a liability. The disclosure isn't a weakness to hide; it's the reveal that makes the whole project land.
